Before you pack

Rules & prohibited items

Travelers are personally responsible for everything in their suitcase when they go through customs. These rules protect them and keep your item from being seized at the airport.

Customer rules Prohibited items What travelers can't carry

Customer rules

These rules apply to everyone who sends or receives an item through Jebena Express.

  1. Describe your item honestly, with a real photo and its true weight.
  2. Leave the item open so the traveler can inspect it. Travelers can refuse any item, for any reason. Don't seal boxes or wrap gifts.
  3. Only send what's allowed: check the prohibited items and the customs rules for your route.
  4. Hand the item over at least one day before the flight, at a time and place you agree with the traveler.
  5. Agree on the fee with the traveler before handover. Any customs duty or tax charged on your item is yours to pay.
  6. Give the pickup code only to the person collecting the item, and only once the item is in their hands.
  7. Be on time and reachable on the phone number you gave, both at handover and at pickup.
  8. Treat travelers with respect. They carry your item in their own luggage and answer for it at customs.
  9. Jebena Express connects people. It isn't a shipping company and doesn't insure items, so don't send anything you couldn't afford to lose.

Prohibited items

Never accepted

  • Weapons, ammunition, and replicas or parts of them
  • Explosives, fireworks, flammable liquids, spray paint and gas canisters
  • Drugs and controlled substances, including khat
  • Counterfeit or pirated goods
  • Ivory and other wildlife products
  • Cash, gold and jewelry (our policy: travelers don't carry money or valuables for others)
  • Sealed packages the traveler can't open and inspect (our policy)

Allowed with care

  • Power banks and spare batteries must go in the traveler's carry-on, never in checked bags.
  • Medicine stays in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription.
  • Food, including coffee, spices and teff, should be sealed and labeled. Fresh meat, dairy, fruit and plants are often stopped at customs.
  • Electronics may need to be declared on arrival, and duty can apply.
  • Several of the same new item can look like goods for sale and may be charged import duty.

Customs rules

The rules depend on which way the item is going.

Rules change and customs officers have the final say. Last checked November 2026. Confirm with the embassy before you travel.

What travelers can't carry

Travelers answer for everything in their suitcase. To protect you, travelers never carry:

Never carry

  • Anything on the prohibited items list, or banned by customs on your route
  • Sealed packages, or anything you haven't opened and inspected yourself
  • Cash, gold or jewelry for someone else
  • Items from anyone you weren't matched with through Jebena Express
  • More than the space you offered, or more than your airline allows
  • Spare batteries or power banks in checked luggage (carry-on only)

Always

  • Inspect every item at handover, and refuse anything you're unsure about.
  • Tap “I've received the item” on your trip page once you have it.
  • Declare items at customs when the rules require it. You're responsible for what's in your suitcase.
  • Keep the item safe, and hand it only to the named person after they give you the pickup code.
