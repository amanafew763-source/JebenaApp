About us

How it works

Shipping a box between Addis Ababa and Washington, London or Frankfurt can cost more than what's inside it and take weeks. Meanwhile, people fly that route every day with half-empty suitcases. We put the two together.

Step 1 of 3 · Post your item
1

Post your item

Tell us what you're sending, roughly how much it weighs, and whether it's going to Addis Ababa or coming from it. A phone for your father, school supplies, coffee and berbere for your sister abroad: if it's allowed through customs, it can go.

2

Get matched with a traveler

We pair you with someone already flying that route. You agree on the fee before anything changes hands, and the traveler can open and inspect the item before it goes in their suitcase.

3

Picked up in person

The traveler lands and the recipient collects the item in person, whether that's your mother in Addis Ababa or you in Frankfurt. Like the first cup of buna poured for a guest, it arrives by hand.

For senders

Send one item, or book a whole bag

  • Send a single item, like medicine, a phone or school supplies.
  • Book a traveler's whole bag: you cover the airline's checked-bag fee plus the traveler's fee.
  • Check the prohibited items list before you post.
For travelers

Earn from the space you're not using

  • List your trip and how much room you have.
  • Choose which requests to accept. You're never assigned an item you didn't agree to.
  • Inspect every item before it goes in your suitcase, and refuse anything you're unsure of.
Before you pack

What travelers can't carry

Travelers are personally responsible for everything in their suitcase when they go through customs. These rules protect them and keep your item from being seized at the airport.

Never accepted

  • Weapons, ammunition, and replicas or parts of them
  • Explosives, fireworks, flammable liquids, spray paint and gas canisters
  • Drugs and controlled substances, including khat
  • Counterfeit or pirated goods
  • Ivory and other wildlife products
  • Cash, gold and jewelry (our policy: travelers don't carry money or valuables for others)
  • Sealed packages the traveler can't open and inspect (our policy)

Allowed with care

  • Power banks and spare batteries must go in the traveler's carry-on, never in checked bags.
  • Medicine stays in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription.
  • Food, including coffee, spices and teff, should be sealed and labeled. Fresh meat, dairy, fruit and plants are often stopped at customs.
  • Electronics may need to be declared on arrival, and duty can apply.
  • Several of the same new item can look like goods for sale and may be charged import duty.
